Our Story

A Land of
Ancient Wonders

Sagada is a municipality in Mountain Province, Philippines, renowned for its hanging coffins, limestone caves, rice terraces, and cool highland climate. Perched at 1,500 meters above sea level, it has been home to the Kankanaey people for over 2,000 years.

Today, Sagada offers adventurers and culture-seekers an extraordinary blend of outdoor thrills and indigenous heritage — from spelunking deep cave systems to witnessing century-old burial traditions still practiced today.

Getting There
Accommodations
Local Rules
🚌

From Manila

Take an overnight bus from Pasay or Cubao to Baguio (6 hrs), then a bus/van to Sagada via Bontoc (4–5 hrs). Total journey: 10–12 hours.

🚐

From Baguio

Sagada-bound buses depart from the Dangwa Terminal. Journey takes about 4–5 hours along scenic mountain roads.

🏡

Where to Stay

Dozens of guesthouses and B&Bs available. Booking ahead is advised during peak season (Dec–Feb). Rates: ₱500–₱2,500/night.

🍽️

Local Food

Don't miss the famous Sagada yogurt, pinikpikan (mountain chicken), and etag (cured pork). Several local restaurants serve hearty highland cuisine.

🌿 Responsible Travel Tips

1
Register at the Sagada Environmental Guides Association (SEGA) office before any cave or trek activity.
2
Hire a local guide — mandatory for cave explorations like Sumaguing. Guides are affordable and very knowledgeable.
3
Do not disturb or remove any rock formations, coffins, or cultural artifacts. Respect local customs and traditions.
4
Bring warm layers! Even in summer, Sagada nights can drop to single digits. Rain gear is essential in wet season.
5
Cash is king in Sagada. There is no reliable ATM in the municipality — bring enough pesos from the city.
